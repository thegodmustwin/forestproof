Inside the experiment

Make the comparison reproducible.

  1. Start with independent reference evidence

    Copernicus Emergency Management Service mapped the 2019 Mount Kenya fire using photo-interpreted fire polygons. The study excluded unanalysed areas and used the mapped burn interior. This reference is independent of the radar rule being tested.

  2. Hold the viewing geometry steady

    Digital Earth Africa Sentinel-1A observations from the same ascending relative orbit supplied three pre-fire dates and a post-fire target. Each assessed centre needed valid provider masks and positive, finite VV and VH power on all four dates.

  3. Build a matched control

    Nearby vegetation controls were screened with Sentinel-2 imagery and excluded the mapped burn neighbourhood. Matching used pre-fire radar power, incidence angle and optical burn ratio. The final comparison retained 144,677 valid pairs.

  4. Apply the rule without changing the labels

    For each band, compare the target with the per-cell median of the three pre-fire values. A flag requires the absolute change to meet the selected dB threshold in both bands. The 2, 3 and 4 dB settings remain research comparisons.

  5. Replay from retained inputs

    The aggregate reproduced byte for byte from the retained inputs. Reproducibility establishes the calculation's repeatability. Establishing a method that performs reliably across environments requires further independent events and stable periods.

Inspect the study denominators

The mapped burn footprint contained 345,356 sampled centres: 344,321 were assessable and 1,035 were unavailable. The strict burn interior contained 313,202 centres, of which 312,353 were assessable. Matching selected 144,743 pairs before scoring; 66 pairs lacked valid target radar for a member. The comparison above uses the remaining 144,677 matched pairs. It does not use the entire burn footprint as its denominator.

What we still need to learn

One fire leaves important questions open.

The study documents a real test and a clear limit. A public alert method needs evidence beyond this result.

  • Smaller changes

    A broad fire in mixed vegetation differs from a small canopy disturbance. Performance on that smaller event remains unmeasured.

  • Seasonal variation

    One target pass and screened controls do not establish behavior across multiple stable periods, seasons and vegetation types.

  • A different detector

    This transparent two-band rule differs from the archived California prototype and the full OPERA temporal detector. Results must stay attached to the method actually tested.
