# ForestProof practitioner validation kit

Prepared 25 November 2026. This is a research protocol and proposed pilot, not evidence that a partner, buyer, grant or measurable outcome exists. Do not send invitations or collect responses without the owner's direction. Keep interview notes and personal details outside this public repository.

## The question

Can a team responsible for a particular landscape assemble and later retrieve a source-linked account of an observation, review, response and follow-up more easily than with its existing tools?

The likely first participant is someone who actually prepares or approves environmental monitoring or restoration reports. The comparison is their current workflow, including tools such as Forest Watcher, EarthRanger, SMART, spreadsheets or email if the participant uses them. Do not assume any tool is inadequate or that an organization needs ForestProof.

## A 25-minute first-use session

Obtain consent for observation and note-taking. Explain that ForestProof is a research prototype. Ask the participant to think aloud; do not coach them through a task until recording whether they could complete it unaided. Do not ask them to enter personal or sensitive case information into the shared demo.

1. **Find evidence:** From the [homepage](https://vmi3711898.contaboserver.net/welcome), find a Geta observation and say when the satellite acquired it. Record completion, time and navigation difficulties.
2. **Interpret a gap:** Open [Discovery](https://vmi3711898.contaboserver.net/discovery) or the [observation comparison](https://vmi3711898.contaboserver.net/visibility#compare-observations). Ask what the coverage percentage means and whether an obscured image proves forest loss or no loss. Correct interpretation: it describes usable observed coverage; unassessed areas remain unknown. Avoid presenting the two historical crops as a change classification.
3. **Trace responsibility:** Complete the [fictional walkthrough](https://vmi3711898.contaboserver.net/tour), identify the review and follow-up, and explain what the unsigned practice dossier can and cannot establish.
4. **Reflect:** Ask what was confusing, what they would do next in their own work, and which information they would need before acting.

Record the version/date, device, task start and end, unaided success, hints, participant's explanation in their own words, errors and next-step difficulty. Record observations rather than inferring ability or impact from clicks. Five to ten sessions are a proposed target, not an achieved sample or statistical guarantee.

## Practitioner interview

Ask about a recent **non-sensitive** example; participants should avoid names, precise locations or confidential records.

- What triggered the work? Who received it, reviewed it and decided what happened next?
- What sources and tools did you use, and how did you judge quality or uncertainty?
- How did you record actions, ownership and follow-up? What was missing when you later assembled a report?
- How long did the process take? Which parts required rework, and how do you know?
- What information cannot be shared, who may approve publication, and what would a safe pilot require?
- Which existing tool already helps? What would an improvement have to do better to be adopted?
- Who is responsible for the budget and decision? What proof would they need? Do not infer willingness to pay from interest.

Five to eight interviews and one interested design partner are proposed targets. Record refusal, disconfirming evidence and alternative explanations alongside positive feedback.

## Measure a real, authorized pilot

Agree a legitimate area, data rights and task before collecting operational records. With an owner-approved test and consistent task boundaries, compare the existing workflow with ForestProof on comparable cases. Record preparation and retrieval time, missing source references, missing decisions or follow-up dates, interpretation errors and rework. Log source availability and personnel cost assumptions. Calculate savings only where measured; include acquisition, infrastructure, staff review and ongoing maintenance in total cost. A small pilot produces indicative results, not proof of ecological impact or statistical significance.
