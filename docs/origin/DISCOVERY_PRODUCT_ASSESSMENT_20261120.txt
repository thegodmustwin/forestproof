# **ForestProof: Product, Search Intelligence, Visual Experience and Go-to-Market Strategy**

Independent strategic review · 20 November 2026

## **Executive conclusion**

ForestProof has crossed an important threshold: **it is no longer just a proposed conservation platform with a synthetic workflow. It now has a documented, deployed public geographic experience using real historical observations, real terrain context and a connected fictional investigation.**

That is meaningful progress.

But I would change the next phase of development quite substantially.

Instead of another broad visual redesign, I would build around three reinforcing capabilities:

1. **The Living Atlas** — the beautiful spatial interface that makes people want to explore.
2. **ForestProof Discovery** — a unified geographic, temporal, scientific and evidence search system that makes the product genuinely useful.
3. **The Evidence Workbench** — the place where an observation becomes a review, a response, a follow-up and eventually a defensible outcome.

The product's organizing idea should be:

> **Find a place. Discover what we know. Understand what we don't. Follow what happened next.**

That sentence unifies geography, discovery, scientific transparency and human accountability.

The most promising commercial opportunity is not a prettier alternative to Global Forest Watch. It is a focused evidence-and-follow-up product for organizations responsible for specific landscapes.

The navigation structure now supports a clear story. But there is still a discontinuity between **exploring real environmental data** and **using ForestProof to accomplish a real conservation task**.

The public visitor can study real coverage, then practice an imaginary response. A practitioner cannot yet take an approved, qualified, real disturbance observation through the deployed production workflow.

That is the difference between an excellent interactive demonstration and a working operational conservation product.

### **What I would fix conceptually**

Stop making the satellite observation itself the central object.

Make **the place and its evidence history** the central object.

A place should have a persistent identity that links its geography, observations, sources, uncertainties, investigations, follow-ups and relevant research.

This creates a much more natural product:

- A visitor finds Geta.
- They explore its observation history.
- They ask which dates had the clearest evidence.
- They examine an observation.
- They see whether any authorized review exists.
- They return later to discover new information or follow-up.

The same mental model works for beginners and practitioners.

It also gives us a foundation for the search engine you're envisioning.

# **2. ForestProof Discovery: the search engine I would build**

This is where I see one of the greatest opportunities.

The ordinary interpretation of search would be a box in the navigation bar that finds pages containing keywords.

That would barely improve ForestProof.

What I would build instead is a **spatiotemporal evidence search engine**.

It would answer questions combining geography, time, source quality, observations, research and authorized decisions.

The search should resolve user intent into structured filters, not fabricate scientific answers.

For example:

**Query:** “Which observation gives us the clearest view of Geta in November?”

A useful result would show the selected acquisition, the assessed share, the study grid, the original source, missing evidence, and a button that opens that observation in its exact atlas context.

**Query:** “What happened after this disturbance was reviewed?”

If no authorized real case exists, the response should say so. It may offer a fictional walkthrough separately, but never pretend that fictional action is a real conservation response.

## **The five search dimensions**

**Geographic discovery**

Search named places, coordinates, authorized areas and currently published footprints. Results immediately reposition the atlas.

Search never treats the provisional Geta rectangle as an official boundary.

**Temporal discovery**

Search observations from specific dates or periods, compare passes, and filter on acquisition date versus verification date.

This could become one of ForestProof's most useful forms of exploration.

**Evidence and quality discovery**

Search by source, sensor, visible coverage, unavailable imagery, uncertainty, provenance or processing version.

Crucially, a coverage-quality search is not the same as a disturbance search.

**Investigation and outcome discovery**

Search authorized case records, review status, responses, follow-up deadlines and connected evidence.

Public searches should index only approved projections. Protected searches enforce permissions before results are returned.

**Scientific and explanatory discovery**

Search methodology, research experiments, satellite products, definitions, technical caveats and reproducibility guides.

A curious beginner can discover evidence and understand its meaning without having to know GIS terminology.

### **Why this could produce strong retention**

The objective is not to make people compulsively scroll. It's to make **each search produce something useful and lead naturally to another worthwhile question**.

A visitor asks about Geta. The system surfaces the relevant dated observations. They compare two passes. They discover that the newer pass has worse usable coverage. They open the source explanation and investigate why. Later, they return to check whether another observation has become available.

That is a natural discovery loop built on genuine information.

For practitioners, the loop is even stronger:

**New evidence → unresolved question → owned action → follow-up date → verified result.**

Returning to the product has a practical purpose.

# **3. The search architecture: powerful without expensive AI infrastructure**

I would not start by building a large language model-powered retrieval platform or a vector database.

The existing dataset is small, structured, mostly deterministic and unusually sensitive to provenance.

A simpler architecture would be more accurate, cheaper and easier to maintain.

### **Recommended technical design**

| Component                       | Initial implementation                                |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------- |
| Public search index             | Generated JSON / SQLite FTS5                          |
| Structured observation search   | Existing validated public report data                 |
| Geographic queries              | Canonical geometry and spatial intersection functions |
| Source search                   | Provenance manifests and approved metadata            |
| Case search                     | Existing protected API with authorization             |
| Search UI                       | Existing frontend and Living Atlas design tokens      |
| Map interaction                 | Existing MapLibre atlas                               |
| Search suggestions              | Curated, deterministic queries                        |
| Natural-language interpretation | Optional later, with structured-query validation      |
| Vector database                 | Not required initially                                |

For the first version, a periodically rebuilt static search index is likely sufficient for public content, while protected cases should be queried separately through the authorized API.

No credentials, protected case material or unpublished coordinates should enter the public index.

### **Search result contract**

Every result should carry enough information to explain itself:

```
{
  "id": "observation:T36MZE:2026-11-08",
  "type": "optical_coverage_observation",
  "title": "Geta — 8 November 2026",
  "grid": "T36MZE",
  "acquired_at": "2026-11-08",
  "source": "Sentinel-2",
  "visible_centres": 6424,
  "sampled_centres": 27722,
  "interpretation": "usable optical coverage",
  "limitations": [
    "Not a forest-loss alert",
    "Research rectangle is provisional"
  ],
  "public": true,
  "source_receipt_id": "<resolved from existing manifest>"
}
```

Illustrative proposed schema. The receipt ID must be resolved from the repository's real manifest, not substituted with a placeholder in production.

Results should always expose a *why this matched* explanation and a source link.

A search for “forest loss” must not silently return optical coverage results as though they were loss detections. It can instead explain that ForestProof currently publishes coverage diagnostics and offer the relevant research.

## **A critical opportunity: search as navigation**

Search should not open an unrelated results page and abandon geographic context.

Selecting a result should update the atlas:

- Camera moves to its valid geographic extent.
- Date selector moves to the correct acquisition date.
- Evidence inspector opens with the matched record.
- Source and uncertainty remain visible.
- The URL can be copied and reproduced.

This is what makes the engine coherent.

The map is the spatial representation of the query. The timeline is its temporal representation. The evidence panel is its scientific representation. The dossier is its accountability representation.

**One query, one underlying truth, several complementary views.**

### **C. The living dossier**

When a user opens a case, they should not see merely a series of text records.

They should see an explorable timeline.

I would prioritize this over an elaborate animated network graph. The lineage needs to remain readable and auditable.

## **The visual principle I would enforce**

**Every major visual interaction should answer a question.**

An orbit animation answers, “Where are we?”

A terrain view answers, “What kind of landscape is this?”

A time slider answers, “How did observations differ?”

A quality overlay answers, “What could the sensor actually assess?”

A dossier timeline answers, “What happened after the evidence was reviewed?”

If an animation answers no meaningful question, it should not be a priority.

# **5. The go-to-market opportunity**

I see three distinct markets, but only one should be the initial commercial focus.

| Audience                   | What attracts them                                         | What makes them return                      | Business role                          |
| -------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| Enthusiasts and students   | Visual exploration, comparisons and scientific explanation | New observations and discoveries            | Public distribution and feedback       |
| Researchers and tinkerers  | Reproducible data, methods, APIs and provenance            | Research datasets and tools                 | Technical credibility and contributors |
| Conservation organizations | Cases, responsibilities, evidence and follow-up reporting  | Active monitoring and accountability duties | Initial paying-customer hypothesis     |

## **The first buyer I would pursue**

A small conservation, watershed, restoration or environmental-monitoring organization that already has responsibility for one defined area.

Not a ministry-wide contract. Not a continent-scale satellite-monitoring agreement.

One organization, one approved landscape, one accountable workflow.

Their potential problem is straightforward:

> “We receive environmental observations and field reports, but it's difficult to consistently determine what needs review, assign responsibility, retain evidence and demonstrate what happened afterward.”

ForestProof should test whether it can reduce that difficulty.

But our differentiation needs to be sharper than “satellite alerts plus field investigations.”

The competitive reality

Forest Watcher already provides offline alerts, field investigations, reporting and team coordination. Its web and mobile tools support areas of interest and assignments.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://fw.globalforestwatch.org\&sz=32)

fw\.globalforestwatch.org+2



That means we cannot honestly claim that the general workflow is unprecedented.

Instead, our hypothesis should be that smaller organizations have unmet needs around **source-transparent evidence histories, interoperable case dossiers, reproducible provenance and funder-facing follow-up reporting**.

Those are plausible differentiators. We need interviews to determine whether they're valuable enough to justify adopting another product.

## **The commercial wedge**

I would frame the first pilot as:

**ForestProof Evidence & Response Pilot**

A bounded institutional engagement that helps an organization review sourced observations in one approved area, retain responsible decisions and produce traceable follow-up records.

We should not quote a subscription price yet. The repository records no validated willingness to pay, procurement requirements or cost of servicing such a customer.

A first pilot should establish whether a customer will use the workflow, what it replaces, how much staff time it consumes or saves, and who controls the budget.

### **Grant readiness is a different sale**

ForestProof can already make a credible prototype-and-research argument:

- There is a deployed public geographic experience.
- There are genuine historical optical measurements.
- Scientific uncertainty and missing information are disclosed.
- Historical radar experiments have reproducible methods and limitations.
- The evidence workflow has a functioning, clearly fictional demonstration.
- Operational launch milestones are explicit.

That is a stronger application than a concept deck alone.

However, a grant application should not claim documented conservation impact, independent operational detection accuracy, institutional adoption or production monitoring.

The strongest funding narrative would be:

**We have built the prototype and demonstrated reproducible environmental-data research. Funding will support qualified source integration, practitioner co-design, secure case operation and field validation.**

This is evidence-based and gives a funder clear milestones.

# **6. Source independence, Golden Economics and the moat**

The provider strategy in your repository is directionally sound.

I would distinguish three types of independence.

| Independence             | Meaning                                                                              | Priority       |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------- |
| Application independence | ForestProof controls the user experience, workflow and evidence model                | High           |
| Integration independence | ForestProof can change qualified sources without redesigning the entire product      | High           |
| Detector independence    | ForestProof develops and operates its own validated disturbance-detection algorithms | Research-stage |

The first two are useful now. The third could eventually be valuable, but it is substantially more expensive and scientifically demanding.

Your own direct-source work illustrates why.

The documented 2019 Mount Kenya research compared radar-derived thresholds against an independent mapped fire event. The 3 dB rule flagged only 7.8% of the matched burn centres and 2.9% of matched controls. That is meaningful experimental work, but not evidence that the rule is ready to detect forest loss operationally. See [direct-source evaluation](https://github.com/thegodmustwin/forestproof/blob/main/docs/DIRECT_SOURCE_EVALUATION.md?utm_source=chatgpt.com).

Current published source descriptions also make clear that RADD, GLAD-L and DIST-ALERT have different geographic and sensor scopes.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://globalnaturewatch.org\&sz=32)

Global Forest Watch



The right choice is not simply the cheapest provider. It is the lowest total-cost pathway that supplies legally usable, useful, recurring observations for the selected operational area.

Costs must include acquisition, processing, storage, quality checking, licensing, staff review and downstream publication. The current Global Nature Watch terms also require attention to attribution and dataset-specific permissions.&#x20;

## **The Still-Fulcrum position**

The economic fulcrum is not the satellite observation.

It is the **trusted transition from uncertain observation to accountable human action**.

That is where organizations may have an operational problem significant enough to pay to solve.

The potential flywheel is:

More legitimate observations reviewed

More structured decisions and follow-ups

**Better evidence of workflow performance**

Improved triage and practitioner confidence

More reasons to use ForestProof

Hypothesized future flywheel; not demonstrated customer growth or defensibility.

**The proprietary asset is not the raw satellite pixels.** It is potentially the permissioned history of how reliable observations were interpreted, how decisions were made, and what subsequent evidence showed.

That history must be earned through actual authorized use. Merely storing many records will not automatically create a competitive moat.

## **9. What I would do next as the founder**

I would run two tracks, not ten.

**Track A: Build the discovery experience.** This is what will make ForestProof visually exceptional, accessible to curious users and more useful than its current collection of pages. It is also a feature that can be built with the public data already available.

**Track B: Prove the institutional workflow with genuine qualified evidence.** This is what could turn ForestProof into a sustainable business rather than an impressive science demonstration.

The strongest go-to-market story will emerge when you can show an organization this simple sequence:

> Here is the landscape you are responsible for. Here are the observations and their uncertainties. Here is what your team reviewed, what it did, and what follow-up evidence supports. Here is the dossier you can reproduce independently.

That is much more compelling than selling “AI-powered forest monitoring.”

The larger vision can ultimately become a searchable **environmental evidence memory**: a governed system that remembers what was observed in a landscape, which questions arose, who reviewed them, and what later evidence showed.

That is a credible application of your Still-Fulcrum and Golden Economics doctrines—but only if the product earns its position through real use, validated data and genuine operational value.

**My strongest recommendation is to make ForestProof's next release a Discovery Engine plus Observation Time Machine, while treating the first genuine institutional pilot as the separate, non-negotiable commercial milestone.**
