# FORESTPROOF — The intelligence system that turns forest loss into accountable action

Kenya's Aberdare crisis × Satellite intelligence × Golden Economics × AlphaZero × Still-Fulcrum doctrine

I think we've found something genuinely worth developing into a crown-jewel company. But the most important discovery from this research is that we should not build another wildfire map, satellite globe or ordinary deforestation detector.

Kenya already has forest-monitoring systems. Global organizations already provide sophisticated satellite alerts. Some are even building predictive AI that forecasts where deforestation is likely to occur next.

Our opportunity is to own the harder problem:

> When a forest disturbance is detected, who must investigate it, what evidence establishes whether the activity is authorized, what action is required, and was that action actually effective?

That is where conservation, environmental law, satellite data, institutional accountability and high-value software converge.

My strongest recommendation is:

# FORESTPROOF

The environmental evidence, intervention and accountability control plane.

Tagline: From satellite warning to verified protection.

Its first operating theater would be the Aberdare water tower, particularly the disputed logging around Geta Forest.

Its long-term market could include forest reserves, water catchments, conservation organizations, forestry operators and institutions responsible for proving environmental compliance across Africa.

## 1. Why this is a real and urgent problem

On October 14, 2026, the Environment and Land Court in Ol Kalou issued conservatory orders temporarily stopping logging in Aberdare Forest, affecting licensed and unlicensed operators pending further directions. Justice Joseph Mugo Kamau is expected to visit Geta Forest on October 23.

The controversy involves competing accounts: petitioners and residents have raised concerns about forest destruction and water security, while forestry authorities and sawmillers have defended some harvesting as managed plantation operations.&#x20;

## 5. The six crown-jewel capabilities

### A. ForestChange — finding the disturbance

Combine multiple observations for an area of interest rather than depending on a single imagery source.

The system might detect a disturbance across successive observations, compare it to its seasonal baseline, check if a second sensor corroborates it, and label the result according to the evidence available.

Critically, forest-loss alerts are not continuously live video. Satellite revisits, cloud cover, spatial resolution and processing delays matter. Selective removal of a few trees may be invisible at 10-metre resolution.

NASA FIRMS, for example, is useful for thermal anomalies and fires—not as a general-purpose logging detector. Its VIIRS fire products use approximately 375-metre pixels, and global availability is often within a few hours of observation.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov\&sz=32)

firms.modaps.eosdis.nasa.gov



### B. ForestLaw — legal and permission intelligence

This is where we begin to separate ourselves from ordinary geospatial dashboards.

The software must understand the relevant legal context for a specific location and date.

For the Aberdare pilot, that includes the October 14 conservatory orders, their scope, their current status and any subsequently issued court directions.

KFS already publishes guidance about timber movement permits and certificates of origin. These are useful compliance inputs, but a missing public record does not establish that no valid permit exists.&#x20;

[image](https://www.google.com/s2/favicons?domain=https://www.kenyaforestservice.org\&sz=32)

Kenya Forest Service



The software should distinguish between verified authorization, verified restriction, unresolved authorization and cases requiring legal review.

### C. EvidenceProof — preserving reliable evidence

Every significant observation becomes an evidence packet containing:

- The source, acquisition time, geographic footprint and satellite/product version.
- The original detection, confidence information and available before/after imagery.
- Relevant land classification and verified legal documents.
- Any authorized field report or corroborating photograph.
- A timestamped history of reviews, corrections, responses and case status.

Evidence objects should be tamper-evident and versioned. Digitally signed reports and cryptographic hashes can help detect alteration; they do not establish that the underlying allegation is true.

### D. InterventionRouter — getting the right information to the right organization

Instead of broadcasting potentially inaccurate accusations, route cases to designated conservation teams or appropriately authorized institutions.

A report might move through:

`NEW → UNDER REVIEW → FIELD VERIFICATION → ACTION RECORDED → FOLLOW-UP → CLOSED / REOPENED`

The product's operational strength comes from reducing the time between evidence appearing and somebody capable of responding receiving a usable case.

### E. CatchmentRisk — understanding environmental importance

A hectare of clearing in one location is not necessarily equivalent in consequence to a hectare elsewhere.

The system could consider forest type, catchment position, proximity to streams, slope, existing land degradation and other environmental attributes when prioritizing investigation.

But we must not claim that one logging event caused a specific change in Nairobi's water supply without hydrological evidence. Water availability depends on rainfall, soils, land cover, abstraction, infrastructure and other factors.

At first, this should be a transparent environmental-priority index, not a purportedly precise water-loss prediction.

### F. OutcomeGraph — learning whether interventions work

This is the long-term crown jewel.

For each alert, preserve the complete relationship:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Disturbance} \rightarrow \text{Evidence} \rightarrow \text{Legal context} \rightarrow \text{Investigation} \rightarrow \text{Intervention} \rightarrow \text{Observed outcome} } \\]

We could eventually learn which types of cases are resolved quickly, which detections generate false alarms, which interventions are followed by continued disturbance, and where investigation resources create the most measurable benefit.

More alerts alone do not make the system smarter. More accurately verified alerts and outcomes might.

## 6. The AlphaZero layer — how do we get better at preventing damage?

A naive system sees 1,000 satellite alerts and sends 1,000 notifications.

An AlphaZero-inspired system asks:

> Which investigation should receive the next scarce unit of human attention, given what we currently know?

Our finite search budget isn't just GPU compute. It includes ranger time, access to remote areas, communications, investigation costs and partner capacity.

The system can maintain a transparent priority over cases using four factors:

\\[ \text{Investigation Priority} \propto \frac{ \text{Expected environmental consequence} \times \text{Evidence strength} \times \text{Actionability} \times \text{Time urgency} }{ \text{Expected investigation cost} } \\]

This is an initial heuristic, not a validated scientific risk formula.

An alert that is uncertain but occurs in a critical catchment under an active legal restriction may deserve investigation before a much larger, already explained plantation-harvesting event.

With field feedback, ForestProof learns how well its prioritization works.

## 7. The Golden Economics: can ForestProof become a business?

This opportunity is different from our software-only authority systems.

The marginal cost of maintaining an additional monitored forest polygon may be small, but the cost of proving and responding to environmental incidents can be significant.

So we should distinguish two businesses:

ForestProof Software: High-margin evidence processing, geospatial analysis, workflow management and reporting.

Forest protection operations: Field inspections, legal work, physical enforcement, restoration and scientific monitoring—performed by qualified partners, not implicitly absorbed into our SaaS economics.

That separation is our Cost-Vector Cancellation.

### Potential customers and their reasons to pay

| Customer segment                                       | Economic value                                                         | Possible business model                 |
| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------- |
| Water utilities and catchment-management organizations | Faster identification and documentation of threats to watershed assets | Annual monitoring / reporting contracts |
| Conservation NGOs and community associations           | Verified cases, field workflow, donor reporting                        | Grants, organizational subscriptions    |
| ESG and environmental-risk teams                       | Evidence of land-cover changes and reported responses                  | Monitoring API / subscription           |
| Forestry and restoration operators                     | Document authorized harvesting, compliance and regeneration            | Per-site compliance subscriptions       |
| Environmental project funders                          | Independent monitoring of funded restoration outcomes                  | Portfolio monitoring contracts          |

I would not begin by trying to sell to everyone.

The sharpest initial buyer hypothesis is an organization with a budget and an existing responsibility to monitor a forest or water catchment, especially one struggling to coordinate observations, field investigations and documentation.

## 9. The technical architecture I'd actually build

Do not begin by recreating God's Eye View's photorealistic global cockpit.

We don't need to render the whole planet to protect one forest.

Start with an operational web map and evidence management system.

| Component             | Recommended starting stack                             |
| --------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| Web application       | Next.js, TypeScript                                    |
| Interactive map       | MapLibre GL JS                                         |
| Backend               | Python FastAPI                                         |
| Geospatial database   | PostgreSQL + PostGIS                                   |
| Data sources          | Global Nature Watch alerts + Copernicus APIs           |
| Geospatial processing | Rasterio, GeoPandas, Shapely                           |
| Scheduling            | Lightweight scheduled jobs                             |
| Evidence storage      | Private S3-compatible object storage                   |
| Alerts                | Email and authorized partner notifications             |
| AI                    | Optional classification and source-grounded summaries  |
| Hosting               | Low-cost VPS or suitable startup-credit infrastructure |

No GPU is required to get the MVP working.

No fine-tuning is required.

No blockchain is required.

For records requiring tamper evidence, conventional signed audit logs and document hashes are a sensible starting point.

When the product demonstrates the need for more sophisticated computer vision, we can test targeted models using labeled data from the actual Kenyan landscapes.

And we should keep the data processing separate from the frontend so new sources or commercial satellite providers can be added without rewriting the application.

That's the Still-Fulcrum geometry: large investments in satellites, sensors and human investigation converge onto a small, important evidence-and-action decision.

It's also our Conservation Audit: we are not claiming to generate environmental protection from nothing. We are helping existing organizations use their expensive observations and limited field capacity more effectively.

And it is AlphaZero-like because every correctly verified case teaches the system where its previous predictions and priorities were useful or mistaken.

### The long-term asset

\\[ \boxed{ \textbf{THE FOREST INTERVENTION OUTCOME GRAPH} } \\]

Over time, that graph could develop a valuable history of forest changes, verified incident classifications, legal circumstances, response processes and outcomes.

Unlike a generic satellite map, it could become a longitudinal operational record that customers rely on for risk management, compliance, monitoring and learning.

We should still avoid treating vulnerable residents, confidential informants or individual field investigators as commodities within that data asset. Protecting identities, sensitive locations and private evidence is central to institutional trust.

## My strategic decision

ForestProof is worth a serious exploratory build, and the Aberdare crisis gives us a compelling first use case. But I would not yet rank it above our existing revenue-oriented crown jewels until it has a credible buyer or implementation partner.

Its strongest initial advantage is that the underlying observation infrastructure largely exists. The difficult, valuable work is connecting observations to verifiable responses, legal evidence and measured outcomes.

That is also what makes it scalable beyond Kenya. Different countries have different forest laws and institutions, but the general workflow remains recognizable:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Observe} \rightarrow \text{Verify} \rightarrow \text{Assign} \rightarrow \text{Act} \rightarrow \text{Prove} \rightarrow \text{Learn} } \\]

The first question I would resolve before commissioning the full Codex build is whether our primary launch objective is an independent public-interest Aberdare monitoring tool or a private institutional software product for conservation organizations and watershed managers.

My preference is the second, with a carefully designed public-interest evidence portal. That gives us a plausible paying customer, protected operational data, measurable conservation impact and a sustainable business that need not depend permanently on donations or hackathon grants.

The satellite sees a change. ForestProof should make it possible to establish what happened, what was done about it, and whether the response worked.

That's the crown-jewel thesis.&#x20;
