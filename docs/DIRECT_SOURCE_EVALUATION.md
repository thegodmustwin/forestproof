# Direct-source evaluation: NASA DIST-HLS and GLAD-L

**Initial access check:** 2026-11-12 UTC. **Evidence and terms reviewed:** 2026-11-13. This is an experimental access assessment, not evidence of a functioning live feed, official Geta boundary, land-cover change, a legal finding, or production readiness. The provisional research box (36.60, -0.49, 36.63, -0.46) is not an authorized operational filter.

## Summary

- NASA OPERA DIST-HLS V1 catalog metadata and a public browse PNG were accessible without login. The owner used Earthdata Login to download four science bands for each of two granules from the November 3 Landsat 8 acquisition. The [two-tile comparison](NASA_DIST_SAMPLE_20261112.md#two-tile-comparison) verified band alignment and complete geometric overlap within the provisional rectangle. Both grids had a November 3 last-assessed date for 722 of 12,432 sampled centres (5.8%). Of those, 237 centres per grid also passed the conservative ForestProof quality screen (1.9% of the rectangle's sampled centres). Both grids describe one acquisition. See the [matching Fmask results](NASA_DIST_SAMPLE_20261112.md#nasa-quality-results-for-november-3). Operational suitability remains unverified.
- GLAD-L's own download page exposes current Google Cloud Storage GeoTIFF paths without using the GNW Data API or running Earth Engine. One 10-degree tile containing the research box responded to an unauthenticated HTTP HEAD request. We did not download this GLAD alert TIFF or query GNW alert records. Current source-specific commercial reuse/retention terms and actual usable pixels remain unverified.
- Both sources remain research candidates. The NASA files support a local sample analysis. ForestProof has no recurring ingestion or verified live feed from either provider.

## NASA OPERA DIST-HLS V1

The public NASA CMR [bounded metadata query](https://cmr.earthdata.nasa.gov/search/granules.json?collection_concept_id=C2746980408-LPCLOUD&bounding_box=36.60%2C-0.49%2C36.63%2C-0.46&page_size=2&sort_key=-start_date) on 2026-11-12 returned footprint-intersecting product entries for Landsat 8 tiles T36MZE and T37MBV. Both have an observation timestamp of 2026-11-03 07:42:55 UTC; the filenames carry production times on 2026-11-07, approximately four days later. This is a point-in-time result, not a latency guarantee. Footprint intersection does not prove valid, cloud-free pixels or detection in the box.

For T36MZE, CMR lists a public `VEG-DIST-STATUS.png` browse visualization. One anonymous bounded GET returned HTTP 200, `image/png`, 357,237 bytes, 1024 × 1024 pixels. It was inspected in memory for format and dimensions only; it is a whole-tile browse image, not georeferenced science data and was not used to infer conditions in the research box. That initial anonymous check downloaded no science raster or alert records. The later authenticated downloads and local inspection are documented in the [sample record](NASA_DIST_SAMPLE_20261112.md).

The same CMR granule metadata points to `lp-prod-protected` GeoTIFF assets. [NASA's OPERA software documentation](https://nasa-opera.github.io/docs/products/) states that downloads require a valid Earthdata Login; [Earthdata](https://urs.earthdata.nasa.gov/documentation/what_do_i_need_to_know) says searches do not require registration and DAAC data downloads do. The owner subsequently confirmed the download worked through their own account. Keep credentials out of chat, logs and Git. [NASA’s Earthdata Login documentation](https://urs.earthdata.nasa.gov/documentation/for_users) describes registration as free. The [product DOI](https://doi.org/10.5067/SNWG/OPERA_L3_DIST-ALERT-HLS_V1.001) and [product specification](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1766/OPERA_DIST_HLS_Product_Specification_V1.pdf) are source references.

The [NASA catalog](https://data.nasa.gov/dataset/opera-land-surface-disturbance-alert-from-harmonized-landsat-sentinel-2-product-version-1-07059) lists public access, displays `other-license-specified`, links to US government works guidance in its metadata, and identifies modified Copernicus Sentinel data. The [NASA-managed AWS Open Data Registry entry](https://github.com/awslabs/open-data-registry/blob/main/datasets/nasa-operal3dist-alert-hlsv1.yaml) lists CC BY 4.0 and controlled Earthdata credentials for S3. These are different descriptions of the same product. Confirm which terms govern the intended access and reuse, including attribution, derived records, retention and redistribution, before external commercial use. The [LP DAAC release announcement](https://forum.earthdata.nasa.gov/viewtopic.php?t=5321) supplies the citation to Hansen (2024), NASA EOSDIS LP DAAC, DOI `10.5067/SNWG/OPERA_L3_DIST-ALERT-HLS_V1.001`, with the actual access date. Preserve the exact granule and layer identifiers with any future derived record.

[DIST-HLS](https://glad.umd.edu/dataset/DIST-ALERT) reports vegetation-cover loss or spectral disturbance relative to a prior baseline. The [algorithm usage constraints](https://lpdaac.usgs.gov/documents/1835/OPERA_DIST_ATBD__V1.pdf) describe multiple possible causes and show how cloud, haze or shadow masking errors can create or hide signals. Keep acquisition, first detection and last quality-assessed dates distinct. A provider status by itself cannot establish forest cover, cause, or a field-verified event.

Both tiles' newest listed acquisition was November 3, 07:42:55 UTC, with production timestamps on November 7. At the time of this check the latest acquisition date was ten calendar days old. ### Local and global catalog follow-up

On 2026-11-14, a public CMR metadata comparison queried acquisition start times from November 4 at 00:00 UTC through November 14 at 08:00 UTC. OPERA DIST-ALERT-HLS V1, HLS L30 v2.0 and HLS S30 v2.0 each returned zero entries for the provisional rectangle and for a wider two-degree box (35.6, -1.5, 37.6, 0.5). With the spatial filter removed, CMR returned 19,906 DIST entries, 7,517 HLS L30 entries and 12,389 HLS S30 entries. The newest listed start time was November 5 for all three collections.

The gap appears in both the upstream HLS metadata and the downstream DIST catalog in these local searches. This narrows the investigation beyond the smaller rectangle, while leaving the actual cause, usable observations and operational cadence unknown. It is insufficient evidence for selecting NASA as the sole launch feed. ## GLAD-L direct route

The [UMD GLAD-L product page](https://glad.umd.edu/dataset/glad-forest-alerts) links to the [GLAD-maintained direct download interface](https://glad-forest-alert.appspot.com/views/download/download-view.html). Its application generates 10-degree tile names. The provisional box falls in `030E_10S_040E_00N`; this is a broad grid tile, not a validated forest boundary. The page describes alert status 0/2/3 as no loss/probable/confirmed and a separate day-of-year file; preserve actual product semantics. It also specifies a scientific citation and warns that observation cadence depends on usable cloud-free imagery.

On 2026-11-12 UTC, an unauthenticated HTTP HEAD request to [the 2026 alert-status tile](https://storage.googleapis.com/earthenginepartners-hansen/GLADalert/C2/current/alert26_030E_10S_040E_00N.tif) returned status 200, content type `image/tiff`, 13,645,212 bytes, `Last-Modified: Wed, 11 Nov 2026 14:28:46 GMT` and `Accept-Ranges: bytes`. HEAD only establishes file metadata: no alert data was downloaded and no valid pixels were checked. The [provider's regional update marker](https://storage.googleapis.com/earthenginepartners-hansen/GLADalert/C2/current/0_GLADalert_update_date.txt) reported `Africa 11_11`, uploaded 2026-11-11 09:57:54; regional freshness does not establish imagery availability in Geta.

The product page provides a citation but the specific GLAD-L alert dataset's machine-readable licence, downstream retention, commercial-use terms, update guarantee and a usable clip remain unverified. The no-charge/CC-BY statement on [GLAD Landsat ARD](https://glad.geog.umd.edu/ard/home) refers to a different reflectance product and must not be silently transferred to GLAD-L alerts. The UMD alert page says these alerts are early event indicators, not reliable loss-area estimates.

## Bounded cost model and next checks

One GLAD-L status file was ~13.0 MiB at this check. As a simple planning calculation, downloading that same size once on each of 30 days would transfer ~390 MiB per month and retain ~390 MiB if every daily copy were kept, before the separately required date/quality layers, compression differences, retries, backups or cloud charges. This is traffic/storage arithmetic, **not a provider fee quotation**. `Accept-Ranges` does not prove the TIFF is cloud-optimized or that a particular small-area read is efficient. Confirm current network, provider terms and actual measured bytes before estimating expenses; no paid account or subscription was created.

For NASA, the remaining research checks are repeat usable coverage and lag across multiple acquisition dates with matching HLS Fmask, and exact product terms for the intended use. For GLAD-L, verify the product licence and bounded machine-readable pixel access. Record product and release, acquisition and receipt times, source bytes and unknown freshness or coverage explicitly. An operational pilot requires an approved area, separate storage, owner authentication and tested recovery. Missing or masked pixels leave disturbance status unknown.
