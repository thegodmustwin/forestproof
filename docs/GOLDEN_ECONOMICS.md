# Golden Economics

## Principle

ForestProof should create greater recurring customer and evidence value than marginal infrastructure cost. It should use existing legitimate scientific and observation infrastructure where practical, while owning the high-value coordination boundary: which observation needs what verification, by whom, under which context, and what was documented afterward.

## P0 operating choices

- Prefer public or licensed data products with documented terms.
- Fetch incrementally; do not reprocess unchanged geography without need.
- Cache permitted derived previews and area statistics.
- Store compact provenance and references rather than mirroring giant image archives.
- Use bounded scheduled jobs, no idle GPU, no Kafka or Kubernetes by default.
- Make field verification a human/customer-controlled activity, not an uncosted software promise.

## Required cost record before a paid dependency

    monthly_fixed_cost
    cost_per_request
    expected_monthly_requests
    storage_cost
    processing_cost
    data_license_cost
    forecast_total_cost

No new recurring paid service is authorized without explicit approval.

## Unit of value

The prospective unit is not alert volume. Track contribution margin per monitored organization, site, and investigation workflow alongside evidence completeness, review latency, and return use. Never claim that a subsidized upstream service has zero cost or that an alert itself represents conservation impact.

## P0 evidence to collect

- Actual provider access, latency, coverage, and usable cost.
- Time saved in triage and evidence assembly.
- Completion rate for investigations and follow-ups.
- Whether a conservation or watershed professional finds a dossier useful.
- Whether workflow history produces legitimate operational learning.
